:'''What's the entrance exam like?'''

:'''Ah. Well... thanks.'''

:'''Combat? But I just want to learn music, not fighting!'''

:'''Oh, no, I'm not ready to take the entrance exam right now.'''

:'''Yes. I'm ready to take the entrance exam.'''

::She gestures toward her throat and mimes singing. Then she gestures toward you.

::'''Sing a short song.'''

::Rebeccah smiles warmly but shakes her head. She performs several gestures you're not familiar with. Seeing your lack of understanding, she sighs dramatically, then mouths a phrase. You watch her lips carefully: she says "NOT YET." (Requires Bard 70)

::'''Ah. Well, I'll come back when I'm a better singer, I guess.'''

:'''Can you tell me more about this school?'''

:'''So the school has a military focus, but once it focused more on music?

:Rebeccah smiles approval. She hands you a sheet of parchment. It seems to be a list of professors, or something like that.

:'''Can I ask you about these professors? It says the headmaster is Leon Dasterre. Who is he?

:Rebeccah smiles and shrugs one slender shoulder. Then she plays a tune on her lute. The song is technically perfect, and ... wait, here lute is smoking! Your alarm recedes when you realize the smoke is purple, and clearly for effect. The song speeds up and her lute catches fire, first purple, then green, then red. The heatless flame grows in size until it's painful to look at her. Then, abruptly, it ends.

:'''That was... insightful. Thank you. What about this name. Professor Ormond Neth?

:Rebeccah nods and picks up her lute. She plays a tavern song, not especially difficult, but warm and inviting. You keep watching and realize something is happening... you are starting to feel and unnatural affection toward Rebeccah. It's not strong enough to control your actions, but it's unmistakable.

:'''Okay! I get it! Stop. Stop. Tell me about this last name, Junior Professor Anaberyl?

:Rebeccah takes a moment to stetch her arms and hands, then plays a lilting fae tune. It starts simple, then adds a second part, and then a third, and then you cannot count them, her fingers an impossible blur of motion. Yet despite the technical complexity, the song itself still seems... simple, somehow. Sincere and straightforward, and desperately upbeat.

:''' I think I get it. This has all been very informative. Thank you.

:'''That's all. Thanks for your time.'''

As the sound builds to a crescendo, her lute lets out a truly horrific sound... and then is suddenly muted.

:After a moment, a quiet lilting tune comes from her instrument, suggesting calm and caring. But as it ends, she repeats the refrain in a minor key. The latent anger is unmistakable.
